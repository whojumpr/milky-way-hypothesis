# Section 4 — Space as a Fabric (The "Rip" Metaphor)

The "rip in space" intuition aligns with how the Bible describes the structural mechanics of the heavens.

## The stretchable universe
The Bible repeatedly says God "stretches out the heavens like a curtain" or a tent (Isaiah 40:22; also Job 9:8, Psalm 104:2, Isaiah 42:5, 44:24, Jeremiah 10:12, Zechariah 12:1 — it is one of the most repeated cosmological images in scripture). Space is pictured not as empty nothingness but as a worked material under tension. `#claim/solid` as a description of the texts; `#claim/interpretation` when mapped onto expanding spacetime.

## The cosmic tear (rend)
In moments of divine entry, prophets pray for God to physically tear the heavens open — "Oh, that You would rend the heavens and come down" (Isaiah 64:1, Hebrew *qara*, to tear). At Jesus' baptism, Mark uses the Greek **schizō** — a violent rip — for the sky opening (Mark 1:10), where Matthew and Luke soften it to "opened" (*anoigō*). `#claim/solid` — and stronger than the foundation originally knew: Mark uses *schizō* exactly twice, at the baptism (1:10) and at the tearing of the temple veil (15:38), bracketing the entire gospel in torn barriers. Josephus (*Jewish War* 5.212–214) records that the temple's outer veil was embroidered with **a panorama of the heavens**. Mark's torn veil *is* a torn sky. This is the single strongest textual asset the hypothesis has — see [[Steelman]].

## The scroll metaphor
At the end, the sky will "roll up like a scroll" (Revelation 6:14, Isaiah 34:4) — the heavens as a coherent sheet that can be rolled, split, and removed. `#claim/solid` as text; the claim that this "uncannily mirrors" general relativity's dynamic spacetime is `#claim/interpretation` — suggestive convergence, not prediction.
