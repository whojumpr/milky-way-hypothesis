# Section 5 — Black Holes as Spiritual Paradigms

Black holes represent the breakdown of ordinary physical description, making them powerful metaphors for the supernatural. **Metaphors** — the audit is strict about that word here.

## The bottomless pit
The Bible references **Abyssos**, the bottomless pit. Black holes are regions where matter falls toward a central point without hitting a floor. `#claim/interpretation` — a legitimate and vivid metaphor; the Greek *abyssos* in its own context refers to the primeval deep / the underworld's depths, not an astronomical object.

## Wandering stars
Jude 13 describes fallen angels as "wandering stars, for whom the blackness of darkness has been reserved forever." `#claim/misreading` when applied to black holes — *planētes asteres* ("wandering stars") was the standard ancient term for **the planets**, whose irregular paths made them emblems of instability. Jude's image is of lights that stray from their courses, not of collapsed stars trapping light. The metaphor can be kept as poetry; it cannot be cited as a biblical anticipation of black holes. See [[Claims Audit]] C8.

## The divine retraction (tzimtzum)
In Lurianic Kabbalah, the infinite God contracts His own light inward to leave a void in which creation can stand. `#claim/solid` as a report of Isaac Luria's doctrine. The claim that this "matches the exact physics" of stellar collapse is `#claim/interpretation`, and a loose one — tzimtzum is withdrawal *to make room*, collapse is concentration *that closes room off*. The direction of the analogy is nearly opposite; keep it as a resonance, not a match.

## The memory bank (holographic principle)
Modern physics conjectures that information falling into a black hole is encoded on its 2D boundary. `#claim/contested` as physics — the holographic principle is a serious, influential conjecture (’t Hooft, Susskind, AdS/CFT), not an established fact about our universe, and the information-paradox debate is live. The mystical comparison (a cosmic record of all history) is `#claim/interpretation`.
