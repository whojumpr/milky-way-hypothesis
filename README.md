# The Milky Way Hypothesis

An Obsidian vault exploring one idea: **that the Milky Way is a tear in the fabric of the heavens through which the light of Heaven leaks into our world** — where the idea came from, what the evidence does to it, and which version of it survives honest examination.

**What this is:** a Christian theological exploration, not a scientific or historical demonstration. The project's method is to keep categories separate — astronomy, scripture, history, folklore, mysticism, gematria — with every claim tagged for what it is ([Rules of Evidence](Rules%20of%20Evidence.md)), so that the exploration can range wide without any category impersonating another. The theological reading that emerges ([Steelman](stress-test/Steelman.md)) is labeled as interpretation throughout.

**Format note:** this repo is an Obsidian vault — internal `[[wikilinks]]` render best in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md) (open the repo folder as a vault). On GitHub, start with this README and the `research/` and `stress-test/` folders, which read fine as plain Markdown.

## Current state of the hypothesis
- **Version A** (literal physical tear): retired — unsupported and observationally excluded in its specified form. Known astrophysical populations adequately account for the observed band; no robust anomalous optical component is detected (resolved into stars since Galileo 1610; ~1.8 billion sources cataloged by Gaia DR3). The band's belt-like form is what an observer embedded in a disk galaxy sees.
- **Version B** (dimensional overlap): held as declared metaphysics — no model, no observational test, no borrowed scientific credibility.
- **Version C** (the band read as a designed *sign*): the live version — a theological interpretation, labeled as such, resting on documented inputs: the verified astronomy of our galactic address; the biblical tearing texts with two independent first-century witnesses to the temple veil as woven cosmos; and a soul-road reception history honestly sized by a 34-tradition survey (a strong plurality across ~4 independent lineages, not a universal).

## Working rules
1. Never commit credentials — no keys, tokens, or `.env` files, in any commit.
2. Every commit is approved before it happens.
3. Every task is logged (`Research Log.md`) and every landed change recorded (`CHANGELOG.md`).
4. Claims keep their evidence tags; nothing contested, pattern-level, or speculative is used as load-bearing support.
5. Living cultural traditions are cited by named community, source, and date; their own destinations and terminology are preserved; comparative coding is ours and implies no endorsement by those communities.
