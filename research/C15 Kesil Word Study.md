# C15 — *Kesîl* (כְּסִיל) = Orion? (research round 1)

Word study researched 2026-08-25. Feeds [[Claims Audit]] C15 and [[Scripture Index]]. Occurrences: Job 9:9; Job 38:31; Amos 5:8; plural *kesîlêhem* Isa 13:10.

## 1. Lexical evidence
BDB (s.v. כְּסִיל II) glosses **Orion**, conceding the connection with *kesîl* I ("fool") is "obscure," and notes "apparently some mythological allusion" to **a giant bound in the skies**. The root *k-s-l* ("be fat/thick") forks into "sluggish/fool" and "stout/strong" — how one root yields both a fool and a burly sky-giant. (HALOT/DCH exact wording **unverified — paywalled**; secondary literature uniformly reports HALOT glossing "Orion." Library check queued.)

## 2. The versions are inconsistent — verse by verse
| Passage | LXX | Vulgate | Targum / Peshitta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job 9:9 | **Hesperus** (evening star!), reordered list | Orion | "giant" (*niphlāʾ* / *gabbārā*) |
| Job 38:31 | **Ōriōn** ("barrier of Orion") | Arcturus | "giant" |
| Amos 5:8 | star names **dropped entirely** | Orion | witnesses conflict |
| Isa 13:10 (pl.) | "Orion and all the adornment of heaven" | "splendor earum" (dodges) | — |

Schiaparelli (1905) read this noise as still converging on Orion: the Aramaic/Syriac/Arabic "giant" (*al-jabbār*) IS the Semitic name of Orion — likely the Nimrod-fettered-in-the-sky tradition, with *kesîl* "fool" as a mocking renaming of the impious strongman, and Job 38:31's "cords" as his fetters. The skeptical reading (Starbuck 2006): the versions are inconsistent and late, so certainty is impossible.

## 3. Alternatives (all minority)
Canopus (Saadia Gaon, ibn Janah, Keil & Delitzsch's "sluggish helper"), Sirius (Niebuhr), Antares (Ibn Ezra). The field's judgment (Jewish Encyclopedia): "the opinion represented by the oldest tradition is generally accepted" — Orion. The Talmud's Berakhot 58b dictum presupposes it. **Open item:** G.R. Driver, "Two Astronomical Passages in the OT," *JTS* n.s. 4 (1953) 208–212 + *JTS* 7 (1956) — the key modern philological treatment, **paywalled; his actual position unverified — do not cite from memory.**

## 4. The plural (Isa 13:10)
"Their *kesîlîm*" shows the word could function **generically** ("their Orions" ≈ the bright constellations — prototype-name semantics, like "hoover" for vacuums). Softens "denotes exactly and only Orion"; doesn't defeat the three singular passages — the LXX of this very verse still reached for Ōriōn.

## 5. "Loosen the cords of *kesîl*" (Job 38:31)
*Mōšekhôt* is a hapax from *mšk* "draw/drag." Favored readings, jointly compatible: (1) the **fetters of the bound giant** (BDB, Schiaparelli, K&D — majority critical reading) and (4) the **cords by which God drags the constellation on its course** (Cambridge Bible; matches v. 32 "lead out the Bear"). The "belt of Orion" rendering (NEB/NKJV) is a modernizing gloss — *mōšekhôt* is plural cords, not a belt. Apologetics-watch note: the "gravitationally bound Pleiades / unbound Orion" claim is refuted on both exegetical and astrophysical grounds (Orion's Trapezium is bound; the Pleiades will disperse) — by creationist astrophysicist Hartnett himself (TJ 18(2), 2004). Never use it.

## Verdict applied
**`#claim/contested` (leaning solid)** — demoted from solid-with-caveat. Vault phrasing: **"traditionally and probably Orion; identification ancient-majority but not demonstrable."** It is the oldest recoverable tradition, the lexicon standard, and the bound-giant imagery fits Orion best; but the earliest witnesses are centuries late and inconsistent, no pre-Hellenistic Hebrew source labels the constellation, and the Isa 13:10 plural shows generic use.
